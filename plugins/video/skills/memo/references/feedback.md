# Two or three feedbacks, never more

When the user records a memo, the idea is usually good already: that is why they recorded it. Too
many remarks are counterproductive (Ihsane, 2026-10-07: "j'ai mis 2-3 parce que je ne veux pas en
faire plus… il ne faut pas qu'il commence à me faire trop de retours, qu'il soit contre-productif").
So each short gets **at most three feedbacks**, only those worth acting on, and often fewer. Zero
is a fine answer for a short that holds.

## The three questions, and only these

Ask each one of every short; write a feedback only when the answer is worth saying.

1. **An idea to add** (`kind: "add"`): could one idea, said in one or two sentences, make this short
   stronger? An example the user surely has, the "why" a viewer will ask, the step the memo skipped.
   Say what to add and where (after which line). Never a whole new section.
2. **A big inconsistency** (`kind: "consistency"`): does the short contradict itself, the memo, or
   something obviously true? Only the big ones: two numbers that do not match, a promise the body
   does not keep, a step that cannot work as said. Small wording is not one. When there is none,
   say nothing (do not write "no inconsistency").
3. **A link to another video** (`kind: "link"`): did the user talk, in another memo, script or video,
   about a parallel subject a viewer of this one would want next? `memo.mjs past` lists everything
   that exists; `to` is one of its ids, word for word. Nothing listed fits, no link. A link says why
   these two go together ("this one says why; that one shows how").

## The lead magnet is not a feedback

Whether every version carries the lead magnet is checked by `memo.mjs check`, not judged: a version
without it is fixed before you end, so it never becomes a remark.

## How a feedback reads

- `title`: a few words, what it is about ("Add the price", "Two different numbers", "Goes with: the derush video").
- `text`: one to three sentences to the user, as "you", in **their** language (the memo's), concrete:
  what, where, why. Your judgement, said as yours.
- A feedback is about the substance, never about the versions' wording: if a version sounds wrong,
  rewrite the version.
